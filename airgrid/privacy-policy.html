Privacy Policy

Last updated: May 28, 2026

AirGrid is an offline-first, peer-to-peer mesh chat app for Android. This privacy policy explains what data AirGrid accesses and how that data is handled. AirGrid is designed to keep messaging local to nearby devices and does not require, transmit, or store messages on a central server.

Summary

  • AirGrid does not collect or transmit your chat messages to servers.
  • Local identity and cryptographic keys are generated and stored on-device.
  • The app requires Bluetooth / Wi‑Fi and may ask for location permissions as required by the Android Nearby Connections API.

What the app stores on your device

  • Messages: Chat messages are stored locally using SQLite. Messages remain on your device unless you or the app delete them.
  • Local identity: A stable node ID (UUID) and display name are stored in app preferences.
  • Cryptographic keys: X25519 keypairs and related key material are generated locally and stored in secure storage (FlutterSecureStorage). Private keys never leave your device.
  • Known contacts: Persisted locally to allow encrypted private messages to be addressed to previously discovered peers.

Permissions

To discover and connect with nearby devices AirGrid may request the following Android permissions at runtime:

  • `BLUETOOTH`, `BLUETOOTH_ADMIN`, and on newer Android versions: `BLUETOOTH_SCAN`, `BLUETOOTH_ADVERTISE`, `BLUETOOTH_CONNECT`
  • `NEARBY_WIFI_DEVICES` (Wi‑Fi Direct / Nearby Connections on newer Android)
  • `ACCESS_FINE_LOCATION` where required by Nearby Connections on some OS versions

If you permanently deny a permission the app exposes a shortcut to the Android app settings so you can review or re-enable permissions.

Networking and third-party services

AirGrid uses the Android Nearby Connections runtime (Google Play Services) for peer discovery and transport. Google Play Services is a third-party component; its behavior and any telemetry it performs are governed by Google's policies. AirGrid itself does not upload chat contents or keys to external servers.

Use of encryption

Private messaging uses X25519 key agreement and authenticated encryption (ChaCha20-Poly1305) where peers have exchanged public keys. Key exchange announcements are rebroadcast on the local mesh to enable encrypted communication. Private keys are stored in device secure storage and are not transmitted to other devices.

Store-and-forward (spool)

When delivering an encrypted private packet to an offline peer, AirGrid may temporarily hold (spool) an encrypted packet locally for limited time so it can be forwarded when the recipient is next seen. Spool entries are bounded by size and time-to-live and are stored only on the device that accepted the spool entry.

Analytics and crash reporting

The default project does not include analytics, usage tracking, or crash reporting. If you add such tools, their behavior will be governed by the libraries you include and their own privacy terms. Review any added dependencies for data collection details.

Data deletion and user controls

You can remove local messages, known contacts, and reset local identity within the app (see Settings). Uninstalling the app typically removes its local data, but Android behavior may vary by device and OS version.

Children

AirGrid is not directed at children. If you are a parent or guardian and believe your child has provided personal data to AirGrid, you can remove the app or clear its local data from device settings.
